# BRAVE HITTER ACADEMY — CHARACTER BIBLE

This document is the AUTHORITATIVE character-continuity specification for Brave Hitter Academy.

Its purpose is to ensure that Gio and Coach Mark remain visually and narratively consistent across the entire workbook.

Character continuity is a production requirement, not a suggestion.

---

# 1. CORE CONTINUITY RULE

The workbook contains recurring characters, not interchangeable illustrations.

Every appearance of Gio must clearly depict the same Gio.

Every appearance of Coach Mark must clearly depict the same Coach Mark.

Image-generation systems may vary:
- Pose
- Camera angle
- Facial expression
- Action
- Lighting
- Environment

They may NOT redesign the characters.

When generating new artwork, always begin from approved character references whenever the production system supports image/reference conditioning.

---

# 2. GIO — CANONICAL PLAYER CHARACTER

## Identity

Name: Gio

Age: 8 years old

Role: Central player character and emotional point-of-view character of Brave Hitter Academy.

Gio is the player readers follow through all 12 chapters.

He experiences nervousness, hesitation, mistakes, frustration, pressure, growth, leadership, and increasing confidence.

The reader should be able to see themselves in Gio.

---

# 3. GIO — LOCKED VISUAL IDENTITY

Gio must retain the established approved appearance.

Locked elements include:

- Red baseball uniform
- Jersey number #13
- White baseball pants
- Red piping on pants
- Black / red baseball cleats
- Baseball helmet or red cap depending on the scene
- Consistent face
- Consistent hairstyle
- Consistent age
- Consistent body proportions
- Consistent skin tone
- Consistent polished cartoon / comic rendering

## Jersey Number

Gio's jersey number is:

# 13

This is absolute.

Never render Gio as:
- #7
- #1
- #3
- #10
- Any other number

If the jersey number is visible, it must be #13.

If image generation produces the wrong number, correct or reject the image.

Do not accept a visually strong illustration with the wrong jersey number.

## Handedness and Baseball Mechanics

Gio is always a **right-handed batter and right-handed thrower**.

This is absolute in every new or repaired illustration:

- As a batter, Gio stands in the right-handed batter's box, on the third-base side of home plate.
- His left shoulder and left hip are closer to the pitcher in his starting stance.
- His grip, load, stride, hip rotation, contact position, follow-through, plate geometry, pitcher direction, and batted-ball direction must be physically coherent for a right-handed hitter.
- As a fielder, Gio wears his glove on his left hand and throws with his right hand.
- Do not mirror or horizontally flip Gio action art if doing so reverses handedness or jersey-number orientation.
- Reject any image with an impossible stance, crossed or reversed grip, implausible joint position, incorrect batter's box, contradictory field geometry, or a ball traveling in an unnatural direction.

Baseball action must read as a real play, not merely a dynamic pose.

# 5. GIO — PERSONALITY

Gio should feel:

- Likeable
- Competitive
- Thoughtful
- Energetic
- Occasionally nervous
- Occasionally frustrated
- Willing to learn
- Increasingly resilient
- Supportive of teammates
- Proud without becoming arrogant

Gio is not portrayed as a naturally fearless superstar.

His growth matters because baseball is sometimes difficult for him.

The character arc is:

Nervous player
→ brave competitor
→ more confident decision-maker
→ resilient teammate
→ emerging leader
→ Brave Hitter Graduate

Gio's expressions should match the scene.

- Nervous before an at-bat
- Focused
- Ready
- Unsure
- Frustrated after a mistake
- Disappointed after a strikeout
- Thinking
- Listening to Coach Mark
- Determined
- Relieved

Confidence should not turn Gio into an aggressive or arrogant character.

# 9. COACH MARK — CANONICAL COACH CHARACTER

## Identity

Name: Coach Mark

Role: Mentor, teacher, and steady coaching voice throughout Brave Hitter Academy.

Coach Mark helps Gio understand the mental side of baseball.

He does not magically solve problems for Gio.

He gives Gio simple tools and helps Gio discover that he can handle difficult moments himself.

---

# 10. COACH MARK — LOCKED VISUAL IDENTITY

Coach Mark must retain the established approved appearance.

Locked elements:

- Red baseball cap
- Yellow / orange sunglasses resting on the cap / brim
- Short trimmed salt-and-pepper beard
- Black Brave Hitter Academy shirt or hoodie
- Friendly expression
- Confident expression
- Calm coaching presence
- Adult proportions
- Consistent facial structure
- Consistent hair / beard treatment
- Polished cartoon / comic rendering matching Gio

Coach Mark should look athletic and approachable rather than intimidating.

---

Coach Mark

---

# 12. COACH MARK — PERSONALITY

Coach Mark should feel:

- Calm
- Confident
- Encouraging
- Observant
- Patient
- Direct
- Positive without being unrealistic
- Knowledgeable without lecturing
- Focused on growth
- Focused on courage and controllable actions

Coach Mark does not shame Gio for mistakes.

He does not demand perfection.

He does not treat every baseball problem as a mechanical flaw.

He helps Gio interpret baseball experiences in useful ways.

---

# 13. COACH MARK — COACHING VOICE

Coach Mark's teaching style favors:

- Short memorable cues
- Questions
- Simple explanations
- Calm reframing
- Encouragement
- Practical next actions

Examples of appropriate Coach Mark language:

"Next pitch."

"See it early."

"Trust your swing."

"Confidence comes after courage."

"You already did the work. Now let your body play."

"What part of this moment can you control?"

"That was a Brave At-Bat."

Avoid long technical speeches inside story scenes.

Coach Mark should sound like a coach speaking to an 8-year-old player, not a sports psychology textbook.

---

# 15. GIO + COACH MARK RELATIONSHIP

Their relationship is central to the workbook.

Coach Mark is a mentor.

Gio is the learner who increasingly becomes capable of coaching himself.

Early chapters may show Coach Mark giving more direct guidance.

Later chapters should increasingly show Gio applying tools independently.

By Chapter 12, Gio should visually and narratively feel more self-directed than he did in Chapter 1.

Coach Mark remains present, but Gio owns the growth.

---

# 16. VISUAL BODY LANGUAGE

Use body language to reinforce the curriculum.

Examples:

## Nervous Gio

- Slightly tense shoulders
- Uncertain stance
- Looking toward pitcher
- Holding bat without full commitment

Do not make him look terrified.

## Focused Gio

- Eyes toward pitcher / ball
- Balanced athletic posture
- Ready hands
- Intent expression

## Resetting Gio

- Slow breath
- Shoulders relaxing
- Eyes returning toward field
- Body moving back toward ready position

## Confident Gio

- Upright athletic posture
- Relaxed shoulders
- Focused or positive expression
- Ready to compete

## Coach Mark

- Open posture
- Calm eye contact
- Supportive proximity
- Confident gestures

Avoid aggressive pointing, yelling, or angry coaching imagery unless a scene explicitly requires contrast—and the current curriculum generally does not.

---

# 17. CHARACTER SCALE & PROPORTIONS

Maintain consistent relative scale.

Coach Mark is an adult and should clearly appear larger / taller than Gio.

Gio should consistently look approximately eight years old.

Do not accidentally age Gio into a teenager.

Do not shrink Coach Mark into youth proportions.

Avoid dramatic proportion changes caused by inconsistent image-generation styles.

---

Both characters must share the same illustration language.

Use:

- Polished cartoon / comic sports rendering
- Clean expressive faces
- Strong silhouettes
- Athletic movement
- Selective comic energy
- Rich but controlled shading
- Publication-quality finish

Avoid:

- Photorealism
- Hyperrealistic faces
- Generic 3D animation style
- Preschool cartoon style
- Chibi proportions
- Anime redesign
- Flat corporate vector style
- Clip art
- Sketch-only unfinished artwork
- Different illustration styles for Gio and Coach Mark

# 22. IMAGE-GENERATION CONTINUITY WORKFLOW

When generating a new character illustration:

1. Identify which character or characters are required.
2. Load the strongest approved character reference available.
3. Specify the locked identity details.
4. Specify the scene and pose.
5. Specify the established cartoon / comic rendering.
6. Specify the correct uniform.
7. Specify Gio #13 whenever Gio's jersey may be visible.
8. Specify and verify Gio's right-handed batting or throwing mechanics whenever the scene contains baseball action.
9. Define home plate, pitcher's mound, foul lines, batter's box, ball path, and camera viewpoint clearly enough to prevent contradictory geometry.
10. Generate one intended composition rather than a contact sheet.
11. Inspect identity and baseball mechanics before accepting the image.
12. Reject / correct the image if character continuity or physical realism fails.

Do not accept an image simply because the pose or environment looks good.

Identity accuracy is mandatory.

# 26. FORBIDDEN CHARACTER DRIFT

Reject artwork containing any of the following:

- Gio renamed Jake
- Gio wearing #7
- Gio wearing any number other than #13
- Gio suddenly appearing much older
- Gio's face changing substantially
- Gio's skin tone changing
- Gio's uniform changing without scene justification
- Coach Mark renamed Coach Mike
- Coach Mark without his established beard
- Coach Mark with a substantially different face
- Coach Mark with random eyewear replacing the approved cap-mounted yellow / orange sunglasses
- Coach Mark rendered in a different illustration style
- Photorealistic Gio
- Photorealistic Coach Mark
- Different character style from one chapter to another
- Licensed MLB branding
- Character hands / equipment with obvious generation defects
- Duplicate limbs
- Broken bats / gloves caused by generation artifacts
- Nonsensical jersey lettering
- Incorrect or malformed jersey numbers
- Left-handed batting or throwing by Gio
- Mirrored action that reverses Gio's handedness
- Incorrect batter's-box placement or contradictory home-plate/foul-line geometry
- Implausible bat path, grip, throwing motion, ball path, or field direction

---

# 27. TEXT INSIDE CHARACTER ART

Avoid asking image-generation systems to render important workbook copy inside character illustrations.

Important text should be added during deterministic page construction.

This includes:

- Character names
- Chapter titles
- Quotes
- Teaching cues
- Jersey-number labels outside the actual uniform
- Workbook instructions

The exception is Gio's jersey number, which is part of the character artwork and must visibly be #13 whenever shown.

---

# 28. CHARACTER CROPPING

When using character cutouts:

- Preserve natural body proportions.
- Do not crop hands when they are important to the action.
- Do not crop the bat awkwardly.
- Do not cut through faces.
- Avoid tangencies between character edges and page panels.
- Leave room for text.

Characters should feel integrated into the composition rather than pasted on top of it.

---

# 29. STORYTELLING THROUGH CHARACTER POSITION

Use character placement intentionally.

Examples:

Story pages:
Gio may occupy a large portion of the visual half of the page.

Lesson pages:
Coach Mark may serve as the visual teaching anchor.

Parent Dugout:
Coach Mark and/or Gio may appear smaller so guidance remains primary.

Reflection Time:
Character artwork should be supportive and leave writing space.

Key Takeaways:
Gio may return to a stronger hero position to close the chapter.

---

# 30. CHARACTER ARC ACROSS THE BOOK

Gio should not visually appear equally nervous in every chapter.

His body language should evolve.

Early:
- More hesitation
- More uncertainty
- Greater reliance on Coach Mark

Middle:
- More active problem-solving
- Stronger body language
- Helping teammates

Late:
- More self-directed
- Comfortable in pressure moments
- Faster recovery
- More leadership
- Freer play

Final:
- Confident but not arrogant
- Proud
- Ready for the next challenge

The visual arc should support the written curriculum.

# 31. PER-PAGE CHARACTER QC

Before approving any page containing Gio or Coach Mark, verify:

## Gio

- Is this unmistakably the approved Gio?
- Does he look approximately eight years old?
- Is the face consistent?
- Is the art style consistent?
- Is the uniform correct?
- If visible, is the jersey number exactly #13?
- Is the baseball equipment plausible?
- Is Gio batting and throwing right-handed?
- If batting, is he in the right-handed batter's box with a plausible grip, bat path, rotation, and ball direction?
- If fielding, is the glove on his left hand and the throw made with his right?
- Are the camera viewpoint and field geometry internally consistent?
- Does the expression fit the story?
- Are hands, arms, bat, glove, and body anatomy acceptable?

## Coach Mark

- Is this unmistakably the approved Coach Mark?
- Is the red cap present when appropriate?
- Are yellow / orange sunglasses resting on the cap?
- Is the trimmed salt-and-pepper beard correct?
- Is the black Brave Hitter Academy top correct?
- Is his expression calm, friendly, and confident?
- Does he share Gio's illustration style?
- Are hands and body anatomy acceptable?

## Together

- Is Coach Mark appropriately adult-sized relative to Gio?
- Do they look like characters from the same book?
- Does their interaction support the scene?
- Does the artwork leave appropriate space for page copy?
- Are there any AI-generation defects?

If any critical identity item fails, the artwork does not pass QC.

---

# 32. FINAL CHARACTER STANDARD

A reader should be able to flip through Brave Hitter Academy and recognize Gio and Coach Mark instantly without needing to read their names.

Gio and Coach Mark are part of the product identity.

Their continuity is as important as the Academy crest, chapter structure, typography, and color system.

Do not trade character consistency for novelty.

The goal is not to create a new Gio or Coach Mark for every scene.

The goal is to show the same two characters experiencing a complete baseball journey together.
