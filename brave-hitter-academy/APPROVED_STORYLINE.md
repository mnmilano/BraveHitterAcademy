CHAPTER 1
BE A BRAVE HITTER
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Courage comes before confidence.

CORE RULE:

"My job is not to get a hit.
My job is to be ready, be brave, and take my best swing."


## STORY — PART 1

Gio loves baseball.

At practice, swinging feels easy. He attacks pitches, laughs with his
teammates, and doesn't worry much about what happens after the ball
leaves his bat.

Games feel different.

When Gio steps into the batter's box and sees the pitcher staring back
at him, everything suddenly feels more important.

His mind starts asking questions.

"What if I strike out?"

"What if I miss?"

"What if everyone sees me mess up?"

The first pitch comes toward the plate.

Gio watches it.

Strike one.

He wasn't fooled by the pitch.

He simply couldn't decide whether to swing.

Coach Mark notices.

Before Gio's next at-bat, Coach Mark tells him:

"You don't have to know what will happen. Just be ready for this pitch."

Gio admits that he feels nervous.

Coach Mark explains that nervousness doesn't mean Gio isn't ready.

Bravery isn't waiting until fear disappears.

Bravery is choosing to compete while the nerves are still there.


## STORY — PART 2

During Gio's next at-bat, he makes himself a promise:

"If it's my pitch, I'm swinging."

The pitch comes.

Gio swings.

And misses.

For a second, Gio expects to feel embarrassed.

Instead, something surprising happens.

The nervous feeling gets smaller.

He had done the thing he was afraid to do.

Another pitch comes.

Gio sees it and swings again.

CRACK.

He hits a hard ground ball.

The infielder makes the play and Gio is out.

Walking back toward the dugout, Gio is disappointed.

Coach Mark meets him and says:

"That was a Brave At-Bat."

Gio looks confused.

He made an out.

Coach Mark explains:

"Don't judge courage by the result. Judge it by whether you were willing
to take your swing."

Gio begins to understand.

He can't guarantee a hit.

But he can choose whether he competes.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. COURAGE FIRST

You don't need to feel confident before you act.

Sometimes the brave action is what creates confidence.


### 2. SWINGS ARE CHOICES

A hit depends on many things.

Choosing to swing at your pitch is something you can control.


### 3. RESULTS AREN'T EVERYTHING

A great swing can result in an out.

A poor swing can sometimes result in a hit.

Brave Hitters judge the quality of their competition, not only the
result.


### WHY IT MATTERS

If Gio only feels successful when he gets a hit, baseball quickly
becomes stressful.

When he learns to value preparation, courage, good decisions, and
committed swings, he has more ways to succeed and more confidence to
build on.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Excellent hitters make outs all the time.

A hard line drive caught by a defender can be a better at-bat than a
weakly hit ball that happens to become a single.

Good hitters learn to separate the quality of their process from the
luck of the result.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Reinforce courage rather than statistics.

Praise:
- Committed swings.
- Good decisions.
- Recovering after a miss.
- Competing while nervous.
- Being willing to attack a hittable pitch.

Useful questions:
- "What felt brave today?"
- "Was there a swing you were proud of?"
- "What did you control well?"

Avoid turning every ride home into an evaluation of hits, strikeouts,
or batting average.

The player's relationship with the parent should always be bigger than
the box score.


## REFLECTION TIME

- When did you take a Brave Swing?
- What did you do well?
- What will you keep working on?
- When did you feel nervous but compete anyway?
- What is something you controlled even when you couldn't control the
  result?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

BE BRAVE.

TAKE YOUR BEST SWING.

CONTROL THE DECISION, NOT THE RESULT.

COURAGE COMES BEFORE CONFIDENCE.

HAVE FUN COMPETING.

CHAPTER 2
SWINGING IS WINNING
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

20 Brave Swings.

CORE IDEA:

Swinging is winning when the swing represents a good decision and
committed competition.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio has started becoming braver at the plate.

But another problem appears.

Sometimes he still waits too long because he is afraid of swinging and
missing.

A hittable pitch goes by.

Then another.

Gio realizes he is searching for a pitch that feels impossible to miss.

Coach Mark asks him:

"How are you going to become a better hitter if you're afraid to take
your swing?"

Gio knows Coach Mark isn't telling him to swing at everything.

A Brave Hitter still makes good decisions.

But when Gio recognizes a pitch he can hit, he needs to attack it.

Coach Mark gives him a new challenge.

For a while, stop counting hits.

Count Brave Swings instead.


## STORY — PART 2

Coach Mark explains the challenge.

Gio will work toward 20 Brave Swings.

A Brave Swing is a committed swing at a pitch Gio believes he can hit.

He might hit it hard.

He might foul it off.

He might miss.

Those results don't change whether the decision was brave.

Gio begins counting.

One.

Two.

Three.

With each committed swing, missing becomes a little less scary.

He stops trying to protect himself from failure.

Instead, he starts competing.

By the time Gio reaches his twentieth Brave Swing, something has
changed.

He isn't asking only:

"Did I get a hit?"

He's also asking:

"Did I see my pitch and take my swing?"


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. GOOD HITTERS MAKE DECISIONS

Waiting forever for the perfect pitch doesn't make hitting easier.

Recognizing your pitch and deciding to attack it does.


### 2. A MISS CAN STILL BE PROGRESS

Missing doesn't automatically mean the swing was bad.

A committed swing at the right pitch can still be a good decision.


### 3. COUNT COURAGE

Instead of counting only hits, Gio can count the times he trusted
himself enough to take his swing.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Fear of missing can make a hitter passive.

Counting Brave Swings gives Gio something he can control and develops
the habit of competing instead of waiting.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Great hitters swing and miss.

They foul pitches off.

They make outs.

Development requires swings.

A hitter who consistently recognizes hittable pitches and attacks them
has something useful to build on.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Praise the decision and commitment before discussing the result.

Useful phrases:
- "I loved that you took your swing."
- "That was a good pitch to attack."
- "Way to stay aggressive."
- "You made a decision and trusted it."

Avoid creating fear around swinging and missing.

The goal is not reckless swinging.

The goal is confident decision-making.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What was your bravest swing?
- Was there a pitch you attacked without hesitating?
- How did you respond after a swing and miss?
- What makes a swing "good" even if you don't get a hit?
- How many Brave Swings can you collect?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

SEE YOUR PITCH.

TRUST IT.

TAKE YOUR SWING.

MISSES HELP HITTERS GROW.

COUNT BRAVE SWINGS, NOT JUST HITS.

CHAPTER 3
FAST PITCH MONSTER
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

See the ball early.

CORE IDEA:

"You can't control how fast they throw.
But you can control how well you see it."


## STORY — PART 1

Gio faces a pitcher who throws harder than anyone he has seen recently.

The first fastball explodes into the catcher's glove.

POP!

Gio's eyes get wide.

In his imagination, the pitcher suddenly becomes the Fast Pitch Monster.

His thoughts begin racing.

"He's too fast."

"I can't catch up."

"I'll never hit that."

The next pitch feels even faster because Gio is already expecting to
be late.

Coach Mark notices what is happening.

Between at-bats he asks Gio:

"When are you seeing the baseball?"

Gio isn't sure.

Coach Mark explains that Gio doesn't need to magically become faster.

He needs to give himself more time.


## STORY — PART 2

Coach Mark gives Gio a plan.

Get ready early.

Watch the pitcher.

Find the release point.

See the baseball as soon as it leaves the pitcher's hand.

Track it toward the plate.

Then trust your timing.

During Gio's next at-bat, he stops thinking:

"He's too fast."

Instead he tells himself:

"Early eyes."

The pitcher throws.

Gio picks up the baseball earlier.

The pitch hasn't actually slowed down.

But it doesn't feel like such a surprise.

Another pitch comes.

Gio sees it early.

Tracks it.

Trusts himself.

And takes his swing.

The Fast Pitch Monster suddenly doesn't look quite so monstrous.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. PICK IT UP EARLY

The sooner Gio sees the baseball, the more information his brain has
about the pitch.


### 2. TRACK THE BALL

Seeing the release is only the beginning.

Gio keeps his attention on the baseball as it travels toward the plate.


### 3. TRUST YOUR TIMING

Once Gio sees the pitch and recognizes something he can hit, he trusts
his preparation rather than panicking because of the velocity.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Faster pitching can make hitters feel rushed.

Seeing the ball earlier helps Gio feel less surprised and gives him a
better opportunity to recognize the pitch and make a confident
decision.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Hitters facing better pitching don't make the pitcher slower.

They improve preparation, visual attention, timing, and decision-making.

Picking up the baseball earlier gives the hitter more useful information.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Avoid reinforcing fear by repeatedly talking about how hard a pitcher
throws.

Instead of:

"Wow, this kid throws really fast."

Try:
- "Get ready early."
- "Find the ball."
- "Early eyes."
- "Trust your timing."

Help the player focus on a useful action instead of the threat.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What does fast pitching make you think about?
- Where can you look to see the baseball earlier?
- What phrase helps you stay focused?
- Did any fast pitch feel easier once you saw it early?
- What can you control against a hard thrower?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

GET READY EARLY.

FIND THE RELEASE POINT.

TRACK THE BALL.

TRUST YOUR TIMING.

SEE IT EARLY. OWN IT.

CHAPTER 4
STRIKEOUT SUPERHEROES
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

NEXT PITCH.

CORE IDEA:

Mental toughness isn't never failing.

It's returning your attention to what you can do next.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio has an at-bat he wants to forget.

He swings through strike three.

Walking back to the dugout, he keeps replaying the pitch.

"I should have hit that."

"Why did I swing at that?"

"That was terrible."

The at-bat is over.

But Gio is still playing it in his mind.

When he returns to the field, he is thinking about the strikeout instead
of the next play.

Coach Mark notices.

He explains that even excellent hitters strike out.

The difference isn't that great players never fail.

The difference is what they do next.


## STORY — PART 2

Coach Mark gives Gio two simple words:

"NEXT PITCH."

A Brave Hitter doesn't have to pretend the strikeout didn't happen.

Gio can feel disappointed.

He can learn something useful.

Then he releases it.

After a miss:

Next pitch.

After a bad call:

Next pitch.

After an error:

Next pitch.

After a strikeout:

Next pitch.

Later in the game, Gio gets another opportunity.

Instead of carrying his previous at-bat into the box, he takes a breath,
looks toward the pitcher, and tells himself:

"Next pitch. My pitch."

Now Gio is competing in the present again.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. STRIKEOUTS HAPPEN

Striking out doesn't mean Gio is a bad hitter.

Failure is part of hitting and part of baseball.


### 2. FEEL IT, LEARN, RELEASE

Gio doesn't have to pretend he isn't disappointed.

He can feel it, learn one useful thing, and move forward.


### 3. RETURN TO THE PRESENT

The previous pitch cannot be changed.

The next pitch is where Gio can compete.


### WHY IT MATTERS

When Gio carries mistakes with him, one bad moment can become several.

Learning to reset protects the next pitch, the next play, and the next
at-bat.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Every elite hitter strikes out.

The best players still experience frustration.

Their advantage is often their ability to recover quickly enough that
the previous failure doesn't steal the next opportunity.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Don't rush to erase disappointment.

Let the player feel it without turning the mistake into a bigger event.

Useful language:
- "That one is over."
- "What did you learn?"
- "What's next?"
- "Next pitch."

Praise recovery speed.

A player who strikes out and returns ready to compete has practiced an
important skill.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What mistake was hard to let go?
- What useful thing could you learn from it?
- How quickly did you return to ready?
- What does "Next Pitch" mean to you?
- What can you do physically to help yourself reset?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

FAILURE IS PART OF BASEBALL.

FEEL IT.

LEARN ONE THING.

LET IT GO.

NEXT PITCH. YOUR PITCH.

CHAPTER 5
SECRET OF LINE DRIVES
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Hard contact.

CORE IDEA:

Good process produces good baseball over time.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio has a simple way of judging every at-bat.

Hit equals good.

Out equals bad.

Then Gio has an at-bat where everything feels right.

He sees his pitch.

He takes a confident swing.

CRACK!

A hard line drive shoots toward the outfield.

The left fielder catches it.

Gio walks toward the dugout frustrated.

"Another out."

Coach Mark asks:

"If that ball landed two feet to the side, would it suddenly become a
better swing?"

Gio thinks about it.

Of course not.

The swing was already good.


## STORY — PART 2

Coach Mark explains that baseball results contain things hitters cannot
control.

A perfectly hit baseball can become an out.

A weakly hit baseball can become a hit.

So Brave Hitters need a better way to judge themselves.

They hunt HARD CONTACT.

Gio begins paying attention to:
- The pitch he chose.
- The commitment of his swing.
- How well he struck the baseball.

He still wants hits.

Of course he does.

But he no longer allows the box score to make every decision about how
he feels.

A line-drive out can still make Gio smile.

He knows what good hitting feels like.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. HARD CONTACT IS A WIN

When Gio sees his pitch and hits it hard, he has done his job even if
a defender catches the ball.


### 2. RESULTS CAN LIE

Not every hit represents a great swing.

Not every out represents a bad one.


### 3. GRADE THE PROCESS

Gio evaluates what he controlled:

His decision.

His swing.

The quality of contact.


### WHY IT MATTERS

If Gio judges himself only by hits, confidence becomes dependent on
luck, defense, and things outside his control.

Focusing on hard contact keeps his attention on repeatable skills.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

A bloop hit and a line-drive out look very different in the scorebook
than they do to a hitting coach.

Over time, consistently striking baseballs hard is far more meaningful
than whether one specific ball happened to find open grass.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Notice hard contact.

Say:
- "You smoked that ball."
- "Great swing."
- "That was a good pitch to hit."
- "Keep doing that."

Don't allow a great swing to become emotionally classified as failure
simply because a defender caught it.

Teach the player to recognize quality.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What was your hardest-hit baseball?
- Did you have an out that was still a great swing?
- What makes contact "good"?
- What parts of an at-bat can you control?
- How would your confidence change if you tracked hard contact?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

HUNT GOOD PITCHES.

TAKE GOOD SWINGS.

HIT THE BALL HARD.

DON'T LET THE SCOREBOOK DEFINE THE SWING.

HUNT HARD CONTACT.

CHAPTER 6
VOICE IN YOUR HEAD
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Replace negative thoughts.

CORE IDEA:

Talk to yourself like a good coach would.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio discovers that sometimes the loudest opponent isn't standing on the
pitcher's mound.

It's the voice inside his own head.

After a miss:

"You can't hit this guy."

After falling behind:

"You're going to strike out."

Before facing a hard pitcher:

"He's too good."

Coach Mark explains that thoughts matter because they influence what Gio
notices, how his body feels, and how confidently he competes.

But Gio doesn't have to believe every thought that appears.


## STORY — PART 2

Coach Mark asks Gio what he would say if a teammate had just missed a
pitch.

Gio would never say:

"You're terrible. You're going to fail."

He'd say:

"You're good."

"Stay ready."

"You've got the next one."

Coach Mark smiles.

"Then why wouldn't you coach yourself the same way?"

Gio begins replacing negative thoughts with short, useful instructions.

"I can't hit him."

becomes:

"See the ball."

"Don't strike out."

becomes:

"Trust my swing."

"I messed up."

becomes:

"Next pitch."

His thoughts don't need to predict success.

They need to help him compete.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. NOTICE THE VOICE

Negative thoughts sometimes happen automatically.

Gio's first job is recognizing them.


### 2. DON'T ARGUE — REPLACE

Instead of fighting with a negative thought, Gio replaces it with a
short instruction that helps him compete.


### 3. BE YOUR OWN COACH

Gio learns to speak to himself with the same confidence and patience
he would give a teammate.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Thoughts influence attention and confidence.

Useful self-talk helps Gio stay focused on actions instead of becoming
trapped in predictions about failure.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Players frequently use short performance cues rather than long internal
speeches.

Simple instructions such as "see it," "stay ready," or "next pitch"
give the brain something useful to do.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Listen for the player's self-talk.

Instead of immediately saying:

"Don't say that!"

Help replace the thought.

Ask:

"What would Coach Mark say right now?"

Or:

"What would you say to your teammate?"

Model calm, useful language yourself.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What negative thought shows up most often?
- What could you replace it with?
- What would you tell a teammate in the same situation?
- Which short phrase helps you compete?
- How does your body feel when your self-talk is strong?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

NOTICE THE THOUGHT.

REPLACE THE THOUGHT.

USE WORDS THAT HELP YOU COMPETE.

BE YOUR OWN GOOD COACH.

CHANGE THE MESSAGE. CHANGE THE MOMENT.

CHAPTER 7
TEAM CAPTAIN
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Encourage teammates.

CORE IDEA:

Brave Hitters make their teammates better.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio has spent a lot of time learning how to manage his own at-bats.

Now Coach Mark asks him to notice something else.

The dugout.

One teammate strikes out and sits alone.

Another makes an error and looks at the ground.

A younger player looks nervous about facing a hard pitcher.

Gio recognizes those feelings.

He has felt all of them himself.

Coach Mark tells Gio:

"Brave Hitters don't only make themselves better. They make the people
around them better."


## STORY — PART 2

Gio begins using what he has learned to encourage teammates.

After a strikeout:

"Next pitch. You've got another one."

Before an at-bat:

"See it early. Trust your swing."

After a hard-hit out:

"That was a great swing."

Something changes.

Gio stops thinking only about his own performance.

He becomes part of creating the kind of dugout where players feel
supported enough to compete bravely.

And Gio notices something unexpected.

Helping teammates makes him feel more confident too.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. LEADERS NOTICE OTHERS

Leadership begins by paying attention to teammates instead of focusing
only on yourself.


### 2. WORDS MATTER

A few encouraging words at the right moment can help a teammate reset
and compete.


### 3. CELEBRATE THE RIGHT THINGS

Gio praises courage, effort, good decisions, hard contact, and recovery,
not only hits and runs.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Baseball is a team sport.

A positive dugout helps players recover from mistakes and play with
greater confidence.

Leadership is something Gio can practice regardless of position,
batting average, or age.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Strong teams have players who remain engaged even when they aren't
batting or making the play.

Leadership can look like encouragement, energy, attention, preparation,
or helping a teammate recover.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Ask about teammates as well as personal performance.

Try:
- "Who did you encourage today?"
- "Who had a great moment?"
- "How did you help your team?"
- "Did someone need you today?"

Praise unselfish behavior.

Leadership shouldn't depend on being the best player.


## REFLECTION TIME

- Who did you encourage?
- When did a teammate need support?
- What words helped?
- How does it feel when someone encourages you?
- What kind of teammate do you want to be?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

NOTICE YOUR TEAMMATES.

ENCOURAGE THEM.

CELEBRATE THEIR SUCCESS.

HELP THEM RESET.

BUILD UP YOUR TEAM.

CHAPTER 8
BRAVE SWING CHALLENGE
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

10 Brave Swings.

CORE IDEA:

Commit to the swing.


## STORY — PART 1

Earlier, Gio learned that swinging can be a victory when he recognizes
his pitch and commits.

Now Coach Mark gives him another challenge.

10 Brave Swings.

This isn't a challenge to get 10 hits.

It isn't a challenge to hit 10 home runs.

It isn't even a challenge to make contact 10 times.

The challenge is to make 10 good decisions and fully commit to them.

Gio starts recognizing that committed swings feel different.

There is no last-second hesitation.

No half-swing because he is afraid to miss.

He sees his pitch and attacks it.


## STORY — PART 2

Gio starts collecting Brave Swings.

Some produce hits.

Some produce foul balls.

Some miss.

One becomes a hard line-drive out.

But Gio counts all of them when the decision and commitment were good.

The challenge changes what Gio notices.

Instead of walking back after every at-bat asking:

"Did I get a hit?"

He asks:

"Did I take my swing?"

Ten Brave Swings become ten pieces of evidence.

Evidence that Gio can make a decision.

Evidence that Gio can commit.

Evidence that Gio can trust himself.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. DEFINE THE WIN BEFORE THE AT-BAT

Gio decides what success looks like before the result happens:

Recognize his pitch and take a committed swing.


### 2. COMMIT COMPLETELY

Once Gio decides to swing, he trusts the decision instead of trying to
protect himself from missing.


### 3. TRACK WHAT YOU CONTROL

Counting Brave Swings trains Gio to notice courage and good decisions
instead of only statistics.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Players become more confident when they repeatedly prove to themselves
that they are willing to compete.

The Brave Swing Challenge gives Gio evidence that he can trust himself.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Hitters improve through committed repetitions.

A player who consistently attacks the right pitches has a useful process
even when individual swings don't produce the desired result.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Help count Brave Swings without turning the challenge into another
statistic the player can fail.

The purpose is evidence of courage.

Ask:
- "Did you recognize your pitch?"
- "Did you commit?"
- "Which swing felt best?"

Celebrate willingness to compete.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What makes a swing brave?
- Which Brave Swing made you proudest?
- Did you commit even when you were nervous?
- What happened after you missed?
- What did your 10 swings prove to you?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

KNOW YOUR PITCH.

MAKE THE DECISION.

COMMIT COMPLETELY.

COUNT COURAGE.

MY PITCH. MY SWING.

CHAPTER 9
PRESSURE IS A PRIVILEGE
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Use your routine.

CORE IDEA:

Same player. Same routine. Big moment.


## STORY — PART 1

Late in a close game, Gio hears his name.

He's up.

Runners are on base.

The crowd gets louder.

His teammates are watching.

Suddenly the walk from the dugout to home plate feels completely
different.

Gio starts thinking:

"This is a huge at-bat."

His breathing changes.

His body feels tight.

He begins trying to create the perfect outcome before he has even seen
a pitch.

Coach Mark reminds him:

"You don't need a special swing because it's a big moment."


## STORY — PART 2

Gio realizes the game situation has changed.

His job hasn't.

He takes a breath.

Looks at his bat.

Steps toward the box.

Finds the pitcher.

The same routine he has practiced in ordinary at-bats brings him back
to something familiar.

The pressure is still there.

Gio doesn't need to fight it.

He can accept that the moment matters and return to his routine.

Coach Mark teaches him that an opportunity to compete in an important
moment is something players can learn to welcome.

Pressure means you have an opportunity.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. PRESSURE MEANS IT MATTERS

Feeling nervous in a big moment doesn't mean something is wrong.

It means Gio cares about what he is doing.


### 2. ROUTINES CREATE FAMILIARITY

Gio uses the same simple routine whether it is the first inning or the
final inning.


### 3. DON'T BECOME A DIFFERENT PLAYER

Big moments don't require Gio to swing harder, think more, or become
someone else.

He trusts the player he already is.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Pressure can pull Gio's attention toward outcomes and consequences.

A routine brings his attention back to familiar actions he can control.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

High-level players rely heavily on routines precisely because game
situations constantly change.

A repeatable preparation process creates familiarity even when the
moment feels unusually important.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Avoid making the moment bigger than it already feels.

Instead of:

"This is huge!"

Try:
- "Same game."
- "Same routine."
- "Trust yourself."
- "Enjoy the opportunity."

Help normalize nerves rather than treating them as a problem.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What situations feel like pressure to you?
- What happens in your body?
- What is your routine?
- What part of the routine helps most?
- How can pressure become something exciting instead of scary?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

PRESSURE MEANS YOU HAVE AN OPPORTUNITY.

BREATHE.

USE YOUR ROUTINE.

DON'T CHANGE WHO YOU ARE.

WELCOME THE MOMENT. TRUST YOUR ROUTINE.

CHAPTER 10
BOUNCE BACK CHAMPION
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Measure recovery.

CORE IDEA:

Measure recovery, not perfection.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio makes an error.

The baseball rolls past him and a runner advances.

His first thought is:

"I can't believe I did that."

He wants the play back.

But baseball doesn't rewind.

A few pitches later, another ball is hit toward Gio.

This time Gio is still thinking about the error.

He reacts late.

Coach Mark later explains that everyone talks about avoiding mistakes.

But mistakes are guaranteed in baseball.

The real skill is recovery.


## STORY — PART 2

Instead of asking:

"Did I make a mistake?"

Coach Mark teaches Gio to ask:

"How quickly did I get back?"

Gio develops a simple bounce-back process.

Breathe.

Learn one thing.

Reset his body.

Find the next job.

Later, Gio makes another mistake.

He is disappointed.

But this time he takes a breath.

"Next play."

He gets ready.

The mistake lasts one play instead of becoming three.

That's progress.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. MISTAKES ARE GUARANTEED

No player gets through baseball without errors, strikeouts, bad swings,
or poor decisions.


### 2. RECOVERY IS A SKILL

Gio can practice getting his mind and body ready again instead of
waiting for frustration to disappear.


### 3. DON'T TURN ONE INTO TWO

The goal isn't perfection.

The goal is preventing one mistake from stealing the next opportunity.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Players who fear mistakes often play cautiously.

When Gio trusts his ability to recover, he can compete more freely
because he knows a mistake doesn't have to control what happens next.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Every player makes errors.

A player's response after the error can influence far more baseball
than the original mistake.

Recovery speed is trainable.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Measure the recovery too.

Instead of focusing only on:

"You made an error."

Notice:

"I loved how quickly you got ready for the next play."

Help the player see recovery as a skill worth celebrating.


## REFLECTION TIME

- What mistake was difficult today?
- How long did it stay with you?
- What helped you reset?
- What was your next job?
- How could you recover even faster next time?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

MISTAKES WILL HAPPEN.

BREATHE.

LEARN ONE THING.

FIND THE NEXT JOB.

RESET. RELOAD. GO AGAIN.

CHAPTER 11
TRUST YOUR TRAINING
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Play free.

CORE IDEA:

Train with purpose. Play with freedom.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio has been working hard.

Batting practice.

Drills.

Tracking pitches.

Working on timing.

Taking Brave Swings.

But during a game, Gio starts thinking about all of it at once.

Where are my hands?

Did I load?

Am I stepping correctly?

Is my bat in the right place?

The more Gio tries to consciously control every piece of his swing, the
less natural the swing feels.

Coach Mark explains the difference between PRACTICE MODE and GAME MODE.


## STORY — PART 2

Practice is where Gio works on skills.

Practice is where he makes adjustments.

Practice is where he thinks about mechanics.

The game is where he trusts that work.

Coach Mark tells him:

"You already did the work. Now let your body play."

At his next at-bat, Gio chooses one simple focus.

See the ball.

That's it.

He lets everything else happen.

His swing feels free again.

Gio realizes trust doesn't mean ignoring preparation.

Trust is what preparation is for.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. PRACTICE WITH PURPOSE

Training is the place to learn, experiment, adjust, and repeat.


### 2. SIMPLIFY IN COMPETITION

During games, Gio reduces his thoughts instead of trying to consciously
control every movement.


### 3. TRUST THE WORK

Repeated preparation gives Gio permission to stop micromanaging himself
and compete.


### WHY IT MATTERS

Overthinking can make familiar movements feel difficult.

Learning when to think and when to trust helps Gio bring his practice
ability into games.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Athletes use practice to build repeatable movement patterns.

During competition, too much mechanical thinking can interfere with
reaction and timing.

Simple external cues often help players compete more freely.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Be careful about giving mechanical instructions immediately before or
during every at-bat.

There is a time for instruction.

There is also a time to compete.

Useful game-day language:
- "Trust your work."
- "See the ball."
- "Play free."
- "You've prepared for this."


## REFLECTION TIME

- What do you think about when you overthink?
- What does a free swing feel like?
- What work have you already done?
- What one simple cue helps you compete?
- When should you work on mechanics, and when should you trust them?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

PRACTICE WITH PURPOSE.

MAKE ADJUSTMENTS IN TRAINING.

SIMPLIFY IN GAMES.

TRUST YOUR WORK.

DO THE WORK. THEN TRUST IT.

ALL-STAR MINDSET
============================================================

CORE LESSON:

Confidence is a choice.

CORE IDEA:

Confidence is a choice strengthened by repeated action.


## STORY — PART 1

Gio looks back at the player he was when he first entered Brave Hitter
Academy.

He remembers being afraid to swing.

He remembers the Fast Pitch Monster.

He remembers strikeouts that followed him back to the dugout.

He remembers negative thoughts.

Pressure.

Mistakes.

Moments when he doubted himself.

Those things haven't disappeared completely.

Gio still gets nervous sometimes.

He still misses pitches.

He still strikes out.

He still makes mistakes.

But Gio is different now.

Because he knows what to do when those moments happen.


## STORY — PART 2

Be brave.

Take his swing.

See the ball early.

Say "Next pitch."

Hunt hard contact.

Coach himself positively.

Build up teammates.

Welcome big moments.

Bounce back.

Trust his training.

Coach Mark explains that this is the All-Star Mindset.

It isn't believing:

"I will always succeed."

It's believing:

"Whatever happens, I know how to compete."

Gio realizes confidence isn't something he has to wait around hoping
will appear.

Every brave decision strengthens it.

Every reset strengthens it.

Every committed swing strengthens it.

Every time he trusts himself, he casts another vote for the player he
is becoming.

Gio steps toward the field.

He's still learning.

He's still growing.

But now he knows how to be a Brave Hitter.


## WHAT GIO LEARNED

### 1. CONFIDENCE IS BUILT

Confidence grows through repeated brave actions, preparation, recovery,
and trust.


### 2. CONFIDENCE DOESN'T REQUIRE CERTAINTY

Gio doesn't need to know he will get a hit.

He needs to believe he can handle the moment and compete.


### 3. USE YOUR TOOLS

The lessons from Brave Hitter Academy give Gio a toolbox he can return
to whenever baseball becomes difficult.


### 4. CHOOSE HOW YOU COMPETE

Gio cannot choose every result.

He can repeatedly choose courage, focus, effort, attitude, and response.


### WHY IT MATTERS

If confidence depends on always playing well, it disappears whenever
baseball becomes difficult.

Gio now has a more durable kind of confidence—one built around how he
prepares, thinks, responds, and competes.


### REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE

Confident competitors don't expect perfection.

They develop routines, reset skills, useful self-talk, preparation
habits, and ways to respond to adversity.

Those tools allow confidence to survive difficult games.


## PARENT DUGOUT

Help the player recognize growth that isn't visible in statistics.

Compare the player to the player who began the Academy.

Ask:
- "What feels easier now?"
- "How do you handle mistakes differently?"
- "What do you know now when you're nervous?"
- "How have you become a better teammate?"
- "What makes you a Brave Hitter?"

Celebrate the person and competitor being developed—not merely the
baseball performance.


## REFLECTION TIME

- Which Brave Hitter skill helps you most?
- What are you most proud of?
- What will you do the next time confidence drops?
- What kind of teammate have you become?
- What does being a Brave Hitter mean to you?


## KEY TAKEAWAYS

BE BRAVE WHEN YOU'RE NERVOUS.

TAKE YOUR SWING WHEN IT'S YOUR PITCH.

SEE THE BALL.

TRUST YOUR WORK.

BOUNCE BACK WHEN BASEBALL KNOCKS YOU DOWN.

BUILD UP THE PLAYERS AROUND YOU.

HAVE FUN COMPETING.

YOU DON'T HAVE TO BE PERFECT TO BE CONFIDENT.

KEEP CHOOSING TO COMPETE.

BE BRAVE. TRUST YOURSELF. PLAY FREE.
