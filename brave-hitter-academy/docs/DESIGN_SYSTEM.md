# BRAVE HITTER ACADEMY — DESIGN SYSTEM

This document is the AUTHORITATIVE visual and production design system for the Brave Hitter Academy workbook.

Its purpose is to prevent visual drift, layout drift, character drift, and generic worksheet redesigns during production.

The approved Chapter 1 pages are the MASTER VISUAL TEMPLATES.

This file controls the visual language, page construction rules, page consistency, and production quality standard for the complete workbook.

---

# 1. CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE

The Brave Hitter Academy should feel like one professionally art-directed sports workbook from beginning to end.

Every page should look like it belongs to the same publication.

The visual system must remain recognizable across:
- Front Matter
- Chapters 1–12
- Confidence Toolkit
- Weekly Missions
- Game Day Journal
- Closing / Graduation

Do not redesign the book section by section.

Change content, illustrations, poses, and baseball situations while preserving the same underlying visual language.

---

# 2. PAGE FORMAT

All interior pages must use:

- Exact size: 11 × 8.5 inches
- Landscape orientation
- Aspect ratio: approximately 1.294:1
- Native landscape composition
- Consistent safe margins on all four sides

Do not create:
- Portrait pages
- Square pages
- Contact sheets
- Multi-page spreads rendered as one page
- Collages that simulate several workbook pages
- Cropped portrait designs stretched into landscape

Each generated or constructed page must be one standalone landscape page.

---

# 3. PAGE NUMBER RULE

Do not add printed page numbers to newly created pages.

Older approved artwork may contain page numbers.

Do not regenerate already approved pages solely to remove those numbers if regeneration would risk changing approved artwork.

During final assembly and cleanup, remove visible legacy page numbers where practical without altering approved artwork.

New pages must omit page numbers from the start.

---

# 4. BINDING RULE

Do not simulate binding in the page artwork.

Do not include:
- Coil graphics
- Spiral graphics
- Rings
- Punch holes
- Binder shadows
- Fake center gutters
- Binding hardware

The physical workbook may ultimately use coil / spiral binding, but the artwork itself should remain clean.

---

# 5. LOCKED COLOR SYSTEM

The visual palette is inspired by classic Cincinnati Reds baseball colors without reproducing copyrighted team branding.

Primary palette:
- Rich baseball red
- Deep black
- Warm cream
- Clean white

Supporting neutrals:
- Soft warm gray
- Charcoal
- Dirt / tan tones when appropriate

Color usage should feel bold and athletic, not corporate.

Red and black should create energy and hierarchy.

Cream and white should create breathing room and readability.

Avoid introducing unrelated dominant colors that make a chapter feel like a different book.

---

# 6. BACKGROUND & PAPER FEEL

The workbook should have a tactile publication feel.

Use:
- Warm cream paper texture
- Subtle grain
- Light distressed print texture
- Baseball dirt texture
- Selective ink / halftone texture

Do not make every page flat pure white.

Do not over-texture pages to the point that copy becomes difficult to read.

Texture should support the page, not compete with it.

---

# 7. TYPOGRAPHY

## Display Typography

Use the locally locked Oswald family for display typography and strong, distressed athletic treatment for:
- Chapter numbers
- Chapter titles
- Major page titles
- Short mantras
- Big takeaway statements
- Section dividers

The display type should feel:
- Athletic
- Bold
- Editorial
- Slightly distressed
- Youth-sports appropriate
- Premium rather than cartoonish

## Body Typography

Use the locally locked Source Sans 3 family for body typography. Body text must remain:
- Highly readable
- Clean
- Large enough for a young reader
- Comfortable in line length
- Well spaced

Do not use distressed display fonts for long paragraphs.

## Hierarchy

A reader should immediately understand:
1. What page type this is
2. The main lesson or story title
3. Supporting copy
4. Callouts
5. Writing prompts

Avoid pages where every element has equal visual weight.

---

# 8. GRAPHIC LANGUAGE

The approved graphic language includes:

- Red painted brush strokes
- Black painted brush strokes
- Rough-edged panels
- Halftone patterns
- Ink splatters
- Baseball dirt / dust
- Speed lines
- Motion slashes
- Torn-strip effects
- Comic burst shapes
- Rough circles
- Scribbles
- Chevrons
- Ribbon accents
- Rough frames
- Small editorial icons
- Black / red footer treatments

Use these selectively.

Do not place every decorative device on every page.

The design should feel energetic, not chaotic.

---

# 9. ILLUSTRATION STYLE

Characters and scenes use a polished cartoon / comic sports style.

The illustration quality should feel:
- Professional
- Expressive
- Cinematic
- Athletic
- Warm
- Mobile-friendly
- Consistent across the entire book

Avoid:
- Generic clip art
- Flat stock-vector characters
- Photorealistic character rendering
- Inconsistent cartoon styles from page to page
- Chibi / exaggerated preschool styling
- Corporate illustration styles

Characters should look like they belong in the same visual universe.

---

# 10. BASEBALL ENVIRONMENTS

Use cinematic baseball environments when appropriate.

Approved environmental directions include:
- Evening / dusk stadium
- Bright daytime baseball field
- Dugout interior
- Sunset practice field
- Batting cage / training environment

Backgrounds should generally contain no unrelated people.

The environment should support the story or teaching moment.

Avoid using the exact same background on every page.

Variation is encouraged as long as the visual world remains consistent.

---

# 11. CHARACTER CONTINUITY

## Gio

Gio is the central player character.

Locked requirements:
- Name: Gio
- Age: 8 years old
- Jersey number: #13
- Red baseball uniform
- White pants with red piping
- Black / red cleats
- Helmet or cap depending on the baseball situation
- Consistent facial features, proportions, and art style

Never:
- Rename Gio to Jake
- Use jersey #7
- Randomly change uniform colors
- Redesign Gio between chapters

## Coach Mark

Locked requirements:
- Name: Coach Mark
- Red cap
- Yellow / orange sunglasses resting on the cap
- Short salt-and-pepper beard
- Black Brave Hitter Academy shirt or hoodie
- Friendly, calm, confident coaching presence
- Polished cartoon / comic rendering consistent with Gio

Never:
- Rename him Coach Mike
- Remove his established appearance
- Randomly redesign facial features
- Turn him into a photorealistic person in workbook interiors

Refer to CHARACTER_BIBLE.md for the full character specification.

---

# 12. BRAVE HITTER ACADEMY CREST

The Academy crest is a recurring identity element.

Use it consistently on:
- Cover
- Chapter openers
- Selected divider pages
- Selected footer treatments
- Graduation / certificate material
- Other places where publication identity benefits from reinforcement

Do not place the crest so frequently that it becomes visual clutter.

Do not redesign the crest unless explicitly instructed.

---

# 13. MASTER TEMPLATE RULE

The approved Chapter 1 pages are the MASTER VISUAL TEMPLATES for the chapter system.

For every chapter after Chapter 1:

- Page 1 references approved Chapter 1 Page 1
- Page 2 references approved Chapter 1 Page 2
- Page 3 references approved Chapter 1 Page 3
- Page 4 references approved Chapter 1 Page 4
- Page 5 references the approved Chapter 1 visual language appropriate for Parent Dugout
- Page 6 references the approved Chapter 1 visual language appropriate for Reflection Time
- Page 7 references the approved Chapter 1 visual language appropriate for a strong chapter close

The goal is template inheritance, not pixel-for-pixel duplication.

Preserve:
- Visual hierarchy
- Information density
- Major composition logic
- Typography scale relationships
- Panel language
- Character / environment balance
- Footer treatment
- Overall page rhythm

Vary:
- Character poses
- Baseball situations
- Scenery
- Supporting icons
- Decorative accents
- Story-specific illustration details

---

# 14. LOCKED SEVEN-PAGE CHAPTER SYSTEM

Every chapter contains exactly seven pages.

## PAGE 1 — CHAPTER COVER

Purpose:
Introduce the chapter and its mental-performance theme.

Visual requirements:
- Large chapter number
- Large chapter title
- Gio hero illustration
- Baseball environment
- Short theme / preview statement
- Strong Academy identity
- Premium cinematic composition

Use the approved Chapter 1 opener as the master.

Do not overcrowd this page with instructional text.

---

## PAGE 2 — STORY — PART 1

Purpose:
Begin the chapter narrative.

Visual requirements:
- Large STORY — PART 1 header
- Strong story title
- Readable narrative copy
- Major Gio illustration / environment
- Optional small supporting callout
- Primarily narrative composition

Do not turn this into an exercise page.

---

## PAGE 3 — STORY — PART 2

Purpose:
Continue and resolve the chapter story.

Visual requirements:
- Large STORY — PART 2 header
- Narrative copy
- Gio action scene
- Coach Mark may appear where appropriate
- Concise quote or teaching banner may be included
- Strong visual continuation of Page 2

Keep the page primarily narrative.

---

## PAGE 4 — WHAT GIO LEARNED

Purpose:
Translate the story into clear mental-performance lessons.

Visual requirements:
- Editorial lesson structure
- Small number of key concepts
- Short explanatory copy
- Coach Mark illustration
- Coach Mark quote / teaching cue where useful
- “Why It Matters”
- “Real Baseball Example”
- Strong bottom takeaway treatment where appropriate

The “Real Baseball Example” is TEXT ONLY.

Do not use MLB player photos.

Do not use identifiable professional-player imagery.

Do not turn the page into a tracker, mission, or checklist-heavy worksheet.

---

## PAGE 5 — PARENT DUGOUT

Purpose:
Help the parent reinforce the chapter lesson.

Visual requirements:
- Parent-focused guidance
- Practical phrases to say
- Useful questions
- Things to reinforce
- Things to avoid when appropriate
- Coach Mark and/or Gio artwork
- Clear editorial organization

Do not combine Reflection Time onto this page.

Do not add unrelated parenting content.

The guidance must tie directly to the chapter’s lesson.

---

## PAGE 6 — REFLECTION TIME

Purpose:
Give the player room to think and write.

Visual requirements:
- Several meaningful prompts
- Generous writing space
- Less visual density than story pages
- Energetic Brave Hitter styling without crowding
- Simple Gio / Coach Mark support illustration where useful

This is a writing page.

Whitespace is functional and intentional.

Do not fill every empty area with decoration.

---

## PAGE 7 — KEY TAKEAWAYS

Purpose:
Create a strong visual conclusion to the chapter.

Visual requirements:
- Few memorable principles
- Chapter mantra
- Strong Gio or Gio / Coach Mark visual
- Bold editorial finish
- Encouraging Brave Hitter message
- Visual sense of progression

Do not turn this page into a Game Day Journal or Weekly Mission.

It should feel like the end of an episode in the same book.

---

# 15. CONTENT DENSITY RULE

Match the information density of the approved master page.

If the source content is too long:
- Tighten wording
- Shorten examples
- Use concise phrasing

Do not:
- Shrink body text excessively
- Add new panels
- Add extra columns
- Fill every inch
- Create tiny text
- Invent a different layout merely to fit copy

The design system takes priority over preserving every unnecessary word.

Meaning must remain intact.

---

# 16. FRONT MATTER DESIGN

Front Matter consists of 13 pages.

It should feel especially polished because it establishes the publication.

Use:
- Strong Academy identity
- More cinematic hero moments
- Character introductions
- Progression map
- Generous writing areas where required
- Strong editorial typography
- Rich but controlled use of environments and texture

The front matter should visually connect directly to the chapter system.

Do not create a polished front matter followed by generic chapter worksheets.

Refer to FRONT_MATTER.md for the approved content.

---

# 17. CONFIDENCE TOOLKIT DESIGN

The Confidence Toolkit contains 6 pages.

The toolkit should feel like a practical mental-performance playbook.

Use:
- Clear tool names
- Strong small visual icons
- Red / black brush labels
- Gio / Coach Mark support illustrations
- Short actionable steps
- High scanability

Avoid making it look like:
- A corporate process diagram
- A medical worksheet
- A plain school handout

Keep the same Academy energy.

---

# 18. WEEKLY MISSIONS DESIGN

Weekly Missions contains:
- 1 section divider
- 12 mission pages

The mission pages need functional repeatability.

Use a consistent mission template across all 12 weeks.

Each weekly page should clearly contain:
- Week number
- Mission title
- Mission statement
- Three challenges
- Monday–Sunday tracking area
- Days completed / 7
- Reflection prompt
- Coach Mark Says

Keep writing and tracking areas usable.

Do not sacrifice functionality for decorative artwork.

The pages should still feel unmistakably Brave Hitter Academy.

---

# 19. GAME DAY JOURNAL DESIGN

The Game Day Journal contains exactly:
- 20 game entries
- 2 pages per game
- 40 pages total

Each game uses the same two-page template:
- Before Game
- After Game + Parent Dugout

This section is intentionally repeatable.

Do not create different journal formats for playoffs, tournaments, championships, or special games.

Do not add dugout-use prompts.

The journal is intended for before and after games.

Keep the visual system consistent while maximizing usability for writing and checkboxes.

---

# 20. CLOSING / GRADUATION DESIGN

The Closing / Graduation section contains 6 pages.

These pages should feel celebratory and emotionally satisfying.

Use:
- Strong Gio / Coach Mark artwork
- Academy crest
- Big editorial typography
- Graduation / completion energy
- Confident use of red and black
- More visual breathing room
- Certificate-style formality on the Certificate of Completion page

The final page should feel like the workbook is ending while the player’s baseball journey is continuing.

---

# 21. WRITING SPACE

When a page asks the player or parent to write:

Provide sufficient space for actual handwriting.

Do not create decorative writing lines that are too tight.

For young players:
- Use fewer prompts with more room
- Favor clarity over quantity
- Leave comfortable margins around writing fields

Whitespace is not wasted space.

It is part of the workbook functionality.

---

# 22. REAL BASEBALL EXAMPLE RULE

“Real Baseball Example” sections remain text-only.

Do not include:
- MLB player photographs
- Professional-player likenesses
- Team logos
- Sports photography
- Photo collages

A small generic baseball icon or decorative baseball element may be used if consistent with the approved master.

The instructional example itself is text.

---

# 23. NO PROFESSIONAL TEAM BRANDING

The workbook may use a Reds-inspired red / black / cream aesthetic.

Do not reproduce:
- Cincinnati Reds logos
- MLB logos
- Licensed uniforms
- Identifiable professional branding

The Brave Hitter Academy must maintain its own identity.

---

# 24. ANTI-DRIFT RULES

The following are NOT acceptable final-page directions:

- Generic ReportLab boxes presented as finished design
- Plain white worksheet layouts
- Corporate presentation slides
- Clip-art workbook pages
- Inconsistent AI-generated page styles
- New layout system for every chapter
- Random fonts from page to page
- Unrelated color schemes
- Excessive empty space caused by poor composition
- Excessively crowded pages
- Tiny body text
- Decorative elements covering writing areas
- Character redesigns
- Random jersey numbers
- Photo-real character insertions
- Contact sheets instead of single pages
- Multi-page images
- Fake coil binding
- Printed page numbers on new pages

If a draft resembles a generic worksheet rather than an illustrated sports publication, it has failed visual QC.

---

# 25. IMAGE GENERATION RULE

Image-generation models may create:
- Character poses
- Baseball environments
- Decorative artwork
- Supporting illustrations
- Story scenes

Image-generation models should NOT be trusted to own:
- Final long-form copy
- Exact typography
- Page numbering
- Final text accuracy
- Multi-page workbook construction
- Curriculum
- Character naming
- Jersey-number consistency
- Repeatable journal / tracker layouts

The production system should separate illustration generation from deterministic final page construction wherever practical.

---

# 26. FINAL PAGE CONSTRUCTION

Execution order and repair behavior are defined only in `WORKFLOW.md` and `../QC_POLICY.md`. This design system supplies the visual and construction constraints those procedures validate.

---

# 27. VISUAL QC CHECKLIST

Every page must pass the following checks.

## Format
- 11 × 8.5 landscape
- Correct orientation
- Safe margins
- No binding graphic
- No printed page number on newly constructed page

## Branding
- Correct Brave Hitter Academy visual language
- Correct crest usage
- Correct red / black / cream / white palette

## Characters
- Gio is Gio
- Gio jersey is #13
- Coach Mark is Coach Mark
- Character art style is consistent

## Typography
- No misspellings
- No broken words
- No AI text artifacts
- No tiny unreadable body copy
- Clear hierarchy

## Content
- Correct chapter / section
- Correct lesson
- No invented curriculum
- No old Jake / Coach Mike references
- No six-page chapter remnants
- No extra missions or trackers inside chapter pages
- “Real Baseball Example” is text only

## Layout
- No clipping
- No accidental overflow
- No crowded edges
- No excessive dead space
- Writing spaces remain usable
- Illustration does not overpower copy

## Visual Quality
- Feels like the approved book
- Does not look generic
- Does not look like a presentation slide
- Does not look like clip art
- Does not introduce a new design system

---

# 28. SOURCE-OF-TRUTH RELATIONSHIP

`CANONICAL.md` defines authority order and content ownership. This file controls visual and technical construction only. Approved Chapter 1 page families and canonical assets implement this system; historical plans, prototypes, and PDFs cannot override it.

---

# 29. FINAL QUALITY BAR

The finished Brave Hitter Academy should feel like a real illustrated youth-sports book that a parent would confidently buy and a young baseball player would want to use.

It should not feel like:
- A document generated from templates
- A school worksheet packet
- A corporate training manual
- A collection of unrelated AI images

The final publication should feel energetic, cohesive, premium, encouraging, and unmistakably Brave Hitter Academy.
