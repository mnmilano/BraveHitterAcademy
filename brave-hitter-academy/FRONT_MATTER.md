# PAGE 4 — WELCOME LETTER

## Title
WELCOME LETTER

Hey Player,

I'm glad you're here.

If you're holding this workbook, it means you're about to start a journey that most baseball players never take.

This workbook is not about becoming perfect. It is not about hitting home runs every game. And it is definitely not about never striking out.

Instead, it's about something much more important.

It's about becoming brave.

Every baseball player feels nervous sometimes. Every baseball player makes mistakes. Every baseball player has games where things don't go the way they hoped.

The players who grow aren't the ones who avoid those moments. They're the ones who learn how to handle them.

Throughout this workbook, you'll meet a player named Gio.

Gio loves baseball, but he struggles with many of the same things young players struggle with. He worries about striking out. He gets frustrated after mistakes. He sometimes loses confidence against harder pitching.

As Gio moves through the Brave Hitter Academy, you'll learn how to take Brave Swings, bounce back after mistakes, handle pressure, build confidence, encourage teammates, and trust your training.

Most importantly, you'll learn that confidence isn't something you wait for. It's something you build.

One swing. One practice. One game at a time.

So grab a pencil. Bring your best effort. And get ready to become a Brave Hitter.

Coach Mark

# PAGE 5 — HOW TO USE THIS WORKBOOK

The Brave Hitter Academy is designed to be used throughout a baseball season. You do not need to finish everything at once. The best way to use this workbook is a little at a time.

## Step 1 — READ
Work through the Academy chapters in order.

Each chapter contains:
- Chapter Cover
- Story — Part 1
- Story — Part 2
- What Gio Learned
- Parent Dugout
- Reflection Time
- Key Takeaways

## Step 2 — PRACTICE
Use the Weekly Missions section to practice the skills from the Academy in real baseball situations.

## Step 3 — USE YOUR TOOLS
Return to the Confidence Toolkit whenever baseball feels difficult. Think of it as your personal mental-skills playbook.

## Step 4 — REFLECT
Use the Game Day Journal before and after games. Before the game, choose your focus and get mentally ready. After the game, think about what went well, what you learned, how bravely you competed, and what you want to carry forward.

The journal is not designed to be completed during the game or in the dugout.

## Step 5 — GRADUATE
At the end of the Academy, complete the Closing / Graduation section. Look back at how you've grown, take the Brave Hitter Oath, and celebrate the competitor you've become.

## Final Message
The goal is not to create a perfect baseball player. The goal is to help you become a confident competitor.

# PAGE 6 — PARENT PLAYBOOK: A NOTE TO PARENTS

Thank you for taking this journey with your player.

Youth baseball is filled with opportunities to teach confidence, resilience, leadership, courage, and perseverance. It can also create pressure, frustration, and fear when the focus becomes too centered on results.

The purpose of the Brave Hitter Academy is to help young athletes build the mental skills that allow them to enjoy the game and compete with confidence.

As a parent, you play an important role in that process.

## What To Praise
- Effort
- Courage
- Focus
- Preparation
- Hustle
- Leadership
- Recovery after mistakes
- Good decisions
- Brave Swings
- Being a great teammate

## Try Not To Center Every Conversation On
- Hits
- Strikeouts
- Batting average
- Errors
- Statistics
- Whether the team won or lost

## Parent Principle
Help your player notice what they can control. The goal is not to remove every hard feeling. The goal is to help them learn how to respond when baseball becomes difficult.

# PAGE 7 — PARENT PLAYBOOK: AFTER THE GAME

## Great Post-Game Questions
- What was your favorite part of the game?
- What are you proud of today?
- What did you learn?
- What was your bravest moment?
- What was your best swing?
- What did you control well?
- Who did you help today?

## The Car Ride Home Rule
After games, allow your child to lead the conversation.

If they want to talk baseball, listen.

If they want to talk about something else, that's okay too.

Sometimes the best coaching happens when no coaching happens at all.

Avoid turning the ride home into an immediate technical review of every at-bat, pitch, error, or decision.

## The Goal
The goal is not to raise a perfect baseball player. The goal is to raise a confident young person who is willing to try hard things, recover from setbacks, support teammates, and continue growing.

# PAGE 8 — THE ACADEMY JOURNEY

Every player starts somewhere.

Some players are afraid of striking out. Some players lose confidence after mistakes. Some players get nervous against faster pitching. Some players hesitate when they should swing. Some players put too much pressure on themselves. Some players simply want to enjoy baseball more.

No matter where you're starting, you're in the right place.

The Brave Hitter Academy was created to help players build something more important than a batting average:

CONFIDENCE.

Not the kind of confidence that appears overnight. The kind that is built one practice, one game, one reset, and one Brave Swing at a time.

You don't need to rush. You don't need to be perfect. You only need to keep moving forward.

# PAGE 12 — MEET GIO

Gio is an 8-year-old baseball player.

He loves baseball. He loves his teammates. He loves hitting baseballs in the backyard.

But Gio struggles with some things too.

Sometimes he gets nervous. Sometimes he worries about striking out. Sometimes he gets frustrated after mistakes. Sometimes he loses confidence when pitchers throw harder than he's used to.

Sound familiar?

That's why Gio's story matters.

Throughout this workbook, you'll follow Gio as he learns new skills and grows as a player. The lessons Gio learns are the same lessons you can learn.

## At The Beginning
Gio struggles with:
- Confidence
- Faster pitching
- Hesitation
- Frustration after mistakes
- Pressure

Gio learns how to:
- Take Brave Swings
- See the ball early
- Say “Next pitch”
- Hunt hard contact
- Coach himself positively
- Encourage teammates
- Handle pressure with a routine

- Trust his training
- Play with durable confidence
